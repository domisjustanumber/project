## Two-level execution for large workstreams

For a large goal with multiple deliverables, use two levels: a global numbered
checklist of observable milestones and a numbered child checklist for the active
milestone. Keep the global plan visible; expand later milestones when their
dependencies and existing implementation are understood. Avoid speculative trees.

Obtain approval for the global scope and order. Present the active child checklist
before executing it. A child expansion that only details already authorized work
does not require redundant approval; obtain approval when it changes scope,
acceptance, ownership, dependencies, or sequencing, or when the human explicitly
requests approval of child plans. Record the approval boundary in the checkpoint.
Approval of one child plan never authorizes unrelated later work.

Use stable identifiers such as global step 3 and child steps 3.1, 3.2, and 3.3.
Freeze approved wording and order at each level. Execute the active child list
sequentially. After each completed child step, reproduce the entire global list
and the entire active child list with updated checkboxes, then continue authorized
work. This is the full-checklist reporting rule for two-level execution; inactive
child lists remain in the maintained plan and need not be repeated every time.

Mark a global milestone complete only when its child steps and its observable
acceptance outcome are verified. Completing a foundation, prototype, or SDK does
not complete the surrounding feature ecosystem. State separately whether the
result is implemented, tested, integrated, published, or ready for human use.
On global completion, retain the completed child list in the maintained record
and present the next expansion without silently adding new scope.

For multiple independent owner workstreams, use multi-workstream-integration.
The coordinator manages dependencies and parallel owners; each owner follows its
own sequential global/child plan. Parallelizing a previously approved serial plan
requires an explicit sequencing amendment. Routine owner chatter stays in owner
chats; consolidated reports show links, both levels of progress, blockers, and the
next useful deliverable. Show full owner checklists when the human requests them.

Checkpoint exact plans at both levels, current child, approval evidence,
dependencies, verification and unfinished parent outcomes. Recover these before
resuming; never infer whole-stream completion from a completed child checklist.
